01 · Why Jarvis exists

The device you speak to should not have to be the device that does the work. Jarvis understands the request, picks the permitted device, explains what it plans to do, does only what is allowed — and says so plainly when something is not built yet.

26

Tools in the registry — files, reminders, memory, search, browser, device status and read-only Google

218

Tests in the suite — 196 passed, 22 skipped on platforms they do not apply to

4

02 · How it works

What happens after you press enter.

01

Understand the request

Jarvis reads what you asked — typed or spoken — and works out what you want and which device it belongs to. If the request is unclear, it asks instead of guessing.

Check Is the target clear? If not, ask — never guess.

02

Choose a permitted device

It checks which of your devices is paired, online, and allowed to do that action. A device that is offline or not permitted is never used silently.

Check Paired, online and permitted? If not, say so.

03

Perform supported actions

Only approved actions run, and anything that changes something asks for your confirmation first. You can stop a running task at any time.

Retry A failed step goes back one step — or stops and reports.

04

Show the result

The result comes back to the device you asked from, with a short record of what was done in the activity history.

Check Result reported, action logged. Nothing hidden.

06 · Safety

Built to say when it can’t — and to prove what it did.

Your account and device are the boundary. Everything Jarvis can do is gated by an allowlist, a confirmation, or a permission — and everything it does is written to a log you can read.

43 / 52

Endpoints behind Google sign-in

Only the health check and the sign-in flow are public.

128-bit

Confirmation IDs

Every state-changing action creates a one-time confirmation ID from 16 random bytes — 128 bits — that you approve or deny. Without it, the action does not run.

5

Apps on the launch allowlist

Only Notepad, Calculator, Paint, File Explorer and Command Prompt can be launched. Anything else is refused, and Windows-only tools report “unsupported” elsewhere.

26 / 26

Tools write to the audit log

All 26 tools run through the registry, which records every call — success or failure — with secrets and file contents redacted. You can export or delete your data.

Cloud by design

Jarvis lives in the cloud: sign in with Google, add your own free Gemini key, and everything — chats, notes, reminders, memories — syncs across your devices. There is no telemetry and no background sync.

Permissions first

Apps can only be launched from an allowlist, files only inside approved folders, and browser actions only on domains you approve. Sensitive fields — passwords, cards, tokens — are never read or filled.

What leaves your account

Chat text and the content you ask to summarise are sent to Gemini using your own Gemini key; search queries go to your chosen search provider; Gmail and Calendar data (local mode) goes to Gemini only when you ask for a summary, and only after you sign in with Google. Passwords are never stored; your Gemini key is stored encrypted and never shown again.
