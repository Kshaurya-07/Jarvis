Deep Horizon: Exploring Europa's Subsurface Ocean

Fictional Science Dossier • Target: Europa (Jupiter II) • Launch Window: October 2032

Mission Overview

The Deep Horizon robotic mission is an international deep-space exploration initiative scheduled for launch in October 2032. Following a six-year gravitational assist trajectory past Venus and Earth, the spacecraft is slated to achieve Jovian orbital insertion in early 2038. Its singular primary objective is to characterize the habitability of Europa, an icy moon harboring a saltwater ocean estimated to contain more liquid water than all of Earth's oceans combined.

Scientific Objectives & Cryogenic Shell

Europa's brittle surface crust is composed of water ice at an equatorial temperature of −160°C. Beneath this crust, tidal flexing induced by Jupiter's gravitational pull generates internal thermal energy, maintaining an expansive global ocean between 60 to 150 kilometers deep.

  • Determine the precise thickness of the outer ice shell (estimated between 15 and 25 km).
  • Identify active hydrothermal vents and subsurface plume vent sites.
  • Analyze volatile organic compounds venting through surface lineae fractures.

Key Science Payload Instruments

The Deep Horizon probe is configured with three primary instruments:

  • Deep-Ice Radar Sounder (DIRS): Operates at 9 MHz and 60 MHz to probe deep below the ice shell and pinpoint the boundary where solid ice transitions into the liquid water ocean.
  • Sub-Plume Mass Spectrometer (SPMS): Samples vapor plumes during close flybys (under 25 km altitude) to measure amino acid and methane concentrations.
  • High-Resolution Cryo-Imager: Multi-spectral optical camera capturing sub-meter surface imagery of chaotic terrain and ice rafts.
